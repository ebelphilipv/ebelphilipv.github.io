Thermal Radiation and Gas Dispersion from an Elevated LNG Flare

How tall must a 1,000 MW emergency flare stack be at Ras Laffan summer conditions, and where does the gas go if the flame is lost? I compared the two API 521 design methods with four CFD models, including a three dimensional burning flame in the 10 m/s design wind with a soot model, and turned the results into a stack height recommendation.

3.66 kW/m²

peak radiation at grade for a 40 m stack in a 10 m/s wind, within 4% of the Chamberlain model

≈ 44 m

recommended stack height, set by the design wind on a dry day

0.16 to 0.24

fraction of heat radiated, still air to wind, bracketing the field value of 0.21

22 to 38 m

downwind reach of the flammable cloud after flame-out; it never reaches grade

Method

  • API 521 point source and Chamberlain solid flame models across wind speeds, humidities and stack heights
  • Steady RANS conserved scalar flame models with equilibrium chemistry and presumed PDF, in still air and in 3D wind
  • Two equation acetylene soot model and a discrete transfer radiation solver with grey gases and turbulence radiation interaction
  • OpenFOAM models of the unignited release, checked against jet correlations and an integral plume model

What it showed

  • The 10 m/s design wind governs the stack height, not still air
  • Physically based CFD confirms the Chamberlain height within about 1 m; the point source is the conservative outlier
  • Wind slows the rich core of the bent flame and raises soot sixfold, so radiation rises with wind
  • Discretisation uncertainty below 2.2% for every still air flame quantity

MSc coursework · Engineering Analysis and Simulation · Coventry University · Group work, December 2023

Structural Analysis and Topology Optimisation of a 10 m Steel Pedestrian Bridge

A 10 m long, 3.5 m wide steel footbridge carrying a crowd of 100 people with bicycles (87.3 kN) and a side wind based on the UK's strongest recorded gust (35.7 kN). We sized the deck, checked its vibration modes, let topology optimisation remove material where it was not working, and smoothed the result into a buildable design.

25%

less material, cutting weight and manufacturing cost

6.13 mm

maximum deflection, down from almost 17 mm and well inside the 20 mm limit

112.9 MPa

peak von Mises stress against a 415 MPa yield, within the safety factor of 1.5

29.4 → 36.8 Hz

first natural frequency raised, moving the bridge further from resonance

Method

  • Mesh convergence study fixed a 200 mm element size, with aspect ratio and skew checks
  • Size optimisation of the deck thickness against the 415 MPa yield stress, minimising mass
  • Modal analysis of the first five natural frequencies before and after optimisation
  • Minimum compliance topology optimisation with a 25% volume reduction and a 20 mm deflection limit, then OSSmooth and remeshing

What it showed

  • Removing a quarter of the steel made the bridge stiffer, not weaker
  • All five natural frequencies rose by 25 to 38%, reducing resonance risk under footfall
  • Aluminium alloy and reinforced concrete compared as lighter options; both would need a thicker deck than steel
  • Material removed at the fixed ends flags where a cheaper secondary material could be used

CFD research project · ANSYS Fluent 2024 R2 · Individual work

Laminar Flow over a NACA 0012 Aerofoil in the Wake of a Cylinder

What happens to an aerofoil that sits directly behind a bluff body, as a rear wing does behind a car or a trailing vehicle does in a slipstream? I modelled a cylinder upstream of a NACA 0012 aerofoil in laminar flow and measured how lift and drag change with angle of attack.

Re 200,000

laminar flow at 9.71 m/s over a 0.3 m chord, Mach 0.028

0°, 5°, 10°

angles of attack, each with its own geometry, mesh and solution

13.8

best lift to drag ratio, reached at 5°

Negative drag

at 0°, the aerofoil is drawn along in the low pressure wake of the cylinder

Method

  • 2D geometry of the cylinder and aerofoil in ANSYS Workbench, in a C-shaped domain for each angle of attack
  • Structured mesh refined around both bodies and along the wake in ANSYS Meshing
  • Laminar, incompressible solution in ANSYS Fluent with no slip walls and a pressure outlet
  • Velocity, pressure, vector and pressure coefficient plots post processed at each angle

What it showed

  • The cylinder wake shields the aerofoil: at 0° and 5° its whole surface sits at negative pressure coefficient
  • Lift rises from 0.14 N to 1.64 N between 0° and 10°, but from 5° to 10° drag grows tenfold while lift grows fourfold, so efficiency peaks at 5°
  • A body in another's wake can see negative drag, the same effect racing cars use when slipstreaming
  • Relevant to rear wings and spoilers that work in the disturbed air behind a vehicle body

MSc dissertation · Coventry University · Individual project, 2024

Design and Prototyping of a Low Cost, Portable UV-C Disinfection Device

Commercial disinfection units cost thousands, weigh up to 75 kg and need mains power, which puts them out of reach of rural clinics. I designed a modular, battery powered UV-C unit in CATIA V5, selected its materials, sized its lamps and battery, costed it, and 3D printed a scaled prototype to prove the assembly.

£379

estimated unit cost, against $3,200 to $4,800 for commercial units

6 kg

battery powered, so it needs no mains supply

12.2 min

theoretical cycle for a 250 sq ft room at a 35 mJ/cm² dose

2 cycles

per charge from a 12 V 3.2 Ah battery, about 26 minutes of run time

Method

  • Reviewed hydrogen peroxide vapour, ozone, dry heat and UV-C methods, and benchmarked five commercial units on price, weight, power and cycle time
  • Morphological analysis to fix the core features: modular body, removable battery pack and a sliding cover panel that blocks UV-C when not in use
  • Modelled every part and the full assembly in CATIA V5, using exploded views to check fits between parts
  • Selected materials in Granta: PVDF and TPE for the body, UV transmitting acrylic for the window and UV blocking polycarbonate for the cover
  • Calculated coverage, dose, cycle time and battery run time, then built a cost model of materials, processing and bought in parts

What it showed

  • A 30 W UV-C lamp at 253.7 nm gives 360° coverage at the front, while four 10 W side tubes keep stray exposure low if an operator has to step in
  • Theoretical coverage of 66.5% of a 1,000 sq ft operating theatre from a single position
  • Unit cost of £378.60: £206.60 materials, £30 processing and £142 bought in parts
  • All parts printed in two days and assembled as designed, proving the modular concept
  • Next step: microbiological testing to ISO 11737-2 to prove the kill rate before clinical use
